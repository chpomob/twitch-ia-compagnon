## Why the late-wake case can no longer lose the capture

`core/actions.py::_observe` stamps `self._clock()` in its `finally`, in the same task step as the provider's return — there is no scheduling point between the module's `return` and the stamp. The plan now pins the module to: read the clock once at the wake → decide → author → return with **no further await**. So on the injected clock the stamp *is* the module's decision instant. The module's last scheduled wake is the edge `expiry − 1 s`, and it never waits on anything scheduled at or after `expiry`; therefore any wake delayed by less than the reserve (e.g. the AC42 `98.0 → 99.6` jump, or a transport answering at the same late instant) still stamps `< expiry` and is adopted by the phase 1 in-time rule (`test_a_confirmation_landing_in_time_survives_a_late_adoption`), never through R10. The reserve is thus exactly the tolerance a delayed wake has, rather than the "latest stamp" the previous plan wrongly claimed. A window already exhausted at entry never sends a request at all.

## What I could not encode

1. **A wake delayed by the whole reserve (≥ 1 s) is not preventable by any module arithmetic**: the stamp belongs to the executor and is taken after the return, so a loop stalled for a full second lands the `success` at `>= expiry` and the phase 1 rule discards it like any late `success`. Making that impossible would require an executor-side change (adopting a `success` by a module-declared decision instant), which AC40/R10 forbid and the ruling did not authorize. I stated this honestly as a limit in decision 1 and P11, and P20 records it in the README beside the executor-cancellation limit.
2. **R10's "Prohibition" paragraph** still reads "no other quantity subtracted from the call deadline … R3's `grace_seconds` is the admission reserve of `capture_too_long` only". Per the instruction to keep every other requirement untouched, I did not edit it; the amended AC41 now authorizes the second subtraction explicitly, but a one-clause cross-reference in R10 ("except the two AC41 names") would remove the residual tension. Arbiter's call.
3. **Exactly 1 s left at entry**: R3 says `skipped:deadline` "when under 1 s remains". AC42/P11 define the window as `≤ 0 → skipped`, so the exactly-1 s case also skips (a zero-length request would be sent and immediately abandoned otherwise). This slightly sharpens R3's letter without contradicting it; R3 itself is unchanged.
4. **Late-answer policy**: an answer observed at or after the edge (even when `now < expiry`) is not adopted — the decision is made on the clock, not on which future won — so the rule is deterministic and testable in either `ManualClock` registration order. That discards a usable text in a ~1 s band; it follows the ruling's "transcription_status reporting the timeout" wording.
